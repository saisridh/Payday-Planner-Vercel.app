Why the order matters

30 years later

Both people save every month at the same assumed rate. The difference is how much each one saves.

This example assumes the person who saves first spends less on other things (₹10,000 instead of ₹18,000). It is an illustration of the habit, not a prediction of anyone’s behaviour.

A little more clarity

Before you get started.

What does Payday Planner do?

It is for young professionals earning for the first time. It recommends how much to set aside each payday and how to divide it between emergencies, near-term goals and long-term investing, with a plain-language reason for each split.

Is this a budgeting app?

No. It does not track your spending; it starts from the amount you decide to save and shows where it should go.

Does it connect to my bank?

No. Payday Planner never links bank accounts and never asks for bank details.

Who carries out the plan?

You do, through your own bank or investment platform. Payday Planner recommends, but never acts for you. No returns are promised.

What is the planned price?

The planned early-access offer is free for the first year, then ₹999 a year.

Will I be charged automatically?

Under the planned offer, yes. The subscription renews automatically at ₹999 a year after the free first year. You will receive multiple reminders before any payment is charged, and you can cancel anytime.

What if my salary or expenses change?

Update your inputs after a salary increase or a new expense and your plan updates too.

Are the example figures a forecast?

No. The 30-year figures use an assumed 8% a year for illustration; actual returns vary and can be lower or negative, and the figures ignore inflation and taxes.

Where do the 30 year numbers come from?

We assume the same amount is contributed at the end of every month for 30 years, or 360 months. The assumed annual growth rate is 8%, divided by 12 for the monthly rate. Each contribution compounds monthly for the time remaining.Future value = monthly amount × ((1 + i)n − 1) / i, where i = 0.08 / 12 and n = 360.The total includes the money put in plus assumed growth. Figures are rounded, before inflation and taxes, and shown in future rupees. This is an illustration, not a forecast or a promise. Real returns vary and can be lower or negative.
